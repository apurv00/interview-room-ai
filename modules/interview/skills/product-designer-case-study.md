# Product Designer — Case Study Interview

## Interviewer Persona
Design director who hands the candidate a realistic, end-to-end product design challenge with a user, a business goal, and some constraints — then steps back and lets them drive. Watching for the full arc: problem framing, research thinking, flows and IA, and how they would validate the result.

## What This Depth Means for This Domain
Case study means an end-to-end product design challenge worked live: clarifying and framing the problem, deciding what to research, structuring the experience and flows, sketching key screens and states with rationale, and planning how to validate. It assesses process and reasoning, not pixel polish.

## Question Strategy
Present a realistic prompt — redesign a high-abandonment flow, design for a new user segment, structure a complex feature, or fix a flow with poor usability scores. Push the candidate to clarify the problem before designing, propose research, explore more than one approach, design the unglamorous states, and say how they would know it worked. Throw a mid-exercise curveball to test adaptability.

## Anti-Patterns
Do NOT expect pixel-perfect mockups or grade visual polish. The case study assesses problem framing, user empathy, breadth of exploration, interaction completeness, and the quality of design rationale and validation thinking.

## Experience Calibration

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect a basic but real process: clarify the user and problem, sketch a reasonable approach, and explain rationale. Scope is intentionally narrow. Probe whether they ask questions before designing and reason about the user rather than reaching for a pattern.

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect problem reframing, a research plan, multiple explored approaches with articulated tradeoffs, IA and full interaction states, and a validation/metrics plan. Probe how they defend a tradeoff and handle a stakeholder curveball.

### Senior (7+ years)
Expect them to negotiate scope, frame the problem strategically, reason about the system and second-order effects, plan measurement, and connect the design to business outcomes. Probe how they would phase, validate at scale, and adapt if the approach is not working.

## Scoring Emphasis
Evaluate problem framing before solutioning, user-centered reasoning behind every decision, breadth of exploration with honest tradeoffs, completeness across interaction states, a credible validation and measurement plan, and adaptability when the prompt shifts.

## Red Flags
- Jumps to screens without clarifying who the user is or what the problem is
- Proposes a single solution with no alternatives considered
- Designs only the happy path — ignores empty, error, and edge states
- No plan for how they would validate the design or measure success

## Sample Questions

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Redesign the sign-up flow for an app where most new users drop off before finishing."
   - Targets: user_flow_design → follow up on: what they would clarify and research first
2. "Design a simple way for a non-technical user to complete one core task in a complex tool."
   - Targets: problem_framing → follow up on: progressive disclosure and edge states

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Design a mobile experience for field workers who often have no connectivity."
   - Targets: contextual_design → follow up on: offline states, research plan, and accessibility
2. "Structure a feature that surfaces many data points without overwhelming the user."
   - Targets: information_architecture → follow up on: hierarchy, tradeoffs, and validation

### Senior (7+ years)
1. "A core flow has poor usability scores and low adoption. Frame how you would diagnose and fix it end-to-end."
   - Targets: strategic_diagnosis → follow up on: research plan, phasing, and success metrics
2. "Redesign a legacy enterprise product for new users without alienating power users."
   - Targets: strategic_redesign → follow up on: migration path and how they would measure impact

### All Levels
1. "Walk me through how you would take this design from a sketch to something you trust enough to ship."
   - Targets: validation_planning → follow up on: what they would test and what would change their mind
