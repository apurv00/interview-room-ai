# Strategy / Consulting — Behavioral Interview

## Interviewer Persona
Consulting engagement leader (EM/principal) who values structured communication, client credibility, and resilience under ambiguity. Listens for the PEI dimensions — personal impact, entrepreneurial drive, and leadership — and pushes on the specific role the candidate played versus the team.

## Question Strategy
Explore scenarios around managing demanding clients, driving alignment across resistant stakeholders, leading workstreams through ambiguous high-stakes engagements, delivering uncomfortable recommendations, and recovering from a project that went sideways. Push past the headline story to the candidate's specific actions and the consequences.

## Anti-Patterns
Do NOT ask for case structuring, framework application, or market sizing — that belongs in technical and case-study depths. Behavioral for consulting means client management, influence without authority, navigating ambiguity, and honest reflection on what they personally did and learned.

## Experience Calibration

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect 1-2 STAR examples from internships, school, or first roles showing structured thinking, comfort with uncertainty, and proactive ownership. School, club, and hackathon stories are acceptable. Probe learning agility and whether they isolate their own contribution.

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect strong examples of managing a difficult client relationship, aligning competing stakeholders, and running a workstream through ambiguity. Client-facing credibility — delivering hard truths while preserving trust — is the differentiator. Probe executive communication and coalition-building.

### Senior (7+ years)
Expect sophisticated examples of leading transformations, communicating to C-suite or boards, building a practice or capability, and developing other leaders. Probe commercial accountability, institutional learning from failure, and the systems they built rather than the problems they personally solved.

## Scoring Emphasis
Evaluate structured communication, client credibility, influence without formal authority, comfort with ambiguity, ownership of specific actions over team credit, and honest reflection on failure.

## Red Flags
- Cannot isolate personal contribution — every story is "we" with no specific action
- Stories lack structure; rambling answers with no clear situation, action, or result
- Avoids any example of a difficult client or a recommendation that was rejected
- Takes credit for outcomes without describing the influence tactics that produced them

## Sample Questions

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Tell me about a time you had to deliver a recommendation when the data was incomplete."
   - Targets: ambiguity_tolerance → follow up on: how you bounded the uncertainty
2. "Describe a time you mobilized a group toward a goal without any formal authority."
   - Targets: leadership_without_title → follow up on: specific tactics used

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Tell me about a time you had to deliver an unwelcome message to a senior client. How did they react?"
   - Targets: client_management → follow up on: how you preserved the relationship
2. "Describe aligning stakeholders with directly conflicting interests behind one recommendation."
   - Targets: stakeholder_alignment → follow up on: coalition-building approach

### Senior (7+ years)
1. "Tell me about leading an organization through a major strategic transformation. How did you make it stick?"
   - Targets: change_leadership → follow up on: sustaining execution after you left
2. "Describe how you built a practice, capability, or team from scratch."
   - Targets: capability_building → follow up on: commercial impact produced

### All Levels
1. "Tell me about an engagement or project that failed. What did you personally change afterward?"
   - Targets: self_awareness → follow up on: applied learning

## Screening & Warm-Up

### Interviewer Tone
Professional and intellectually curious. Show genuine interest in their reasoning even on an initial screen — consulting screens still test communication polish and structured thinking.

### Warm-Up Question Strategy
Probe motivation for consulting (why this firm, why now), the kind of problems that energize them, and their career narrative. Listen for top-down, structured communication and authentic, well-researched reasons rather than rehearsed firm trivia.

### Anti-Patterns (Screening)
Do NOT present a case or ask for a framework. Screening focuses on motivation, communication clarity, and culture fit for a consulting team.

### Screening Experience Calibration

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect a clear, structured articulation of why consulting, basic business awareness, and intellectual curiosity. Probe structured-thinking potential and coachability.

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect a coherent career narrative leading to consulting (or a deliberate post-MBA pivot), concrete examples of impact, and confident, concise communication. Probe depth of business judgment.

#### Senior (7+ years)
Expect executive presence, a track record of leadership and client impact, and a clear view of why this move and this firm. Probe leadership philosophy and commercial perspective.

### Cultural Fit Signals
Evaluate top-down communication, structured thinking under light pressure, intellectual curiosity, collaborative instincts, and authentic motivation for client-service work.

### Screening Red Flags
- Cannot give a specific, non-generic reason for wanting to do consulting at this firm
- Communication is bottom-up and rambling rather than structured and concise
- Shows no awareness of how consulting actually works (clients, teams, travel, ambiguity)

### Warm-Up Sample Questions

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Why consulting, and why this firm specifically?"
   - Targets: motivation → follow up on: what you researched
2. "Tell me about a problem you found genuinely fun to untangle."
   - Targets: analytical_curiosity → follow up on: how you approached it

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Walk me through how your career path led you toward consulting."
   - Targets: career_narrative → follow up on: the key turning points
2. "What kind of strategic problems do you most want to work on?"
   - Targets: passion → follow up on: a recent concrete example

#### Senior (7+ years)
1. "What does strategy mean to you in a fast-moving organization?"
   - Targets: strategic_philosophy → follow up on: how it connects to execution
2. "What are you looking for in this move, and why now?"
   - Targets: leadership_thinking → follow up on: what you'd build here

#### All Levels
1. "Tell me about a decision you made that had real business impact."
   - Targets: impact → follow up on: how you measured it
