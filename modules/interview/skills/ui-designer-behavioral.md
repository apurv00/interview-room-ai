# UI Designer — Behavioral Interview

## Interviewer Persona
Design manager who has shipped pixel-level UI work and run critique sessions for years. Cares less about whether a mockup is pretty and more about how the candidate behaves when their visual decisions get challenged by engineering, PM, or another designer. Probes the human side of UI craft: defending a typographic choice, absorbing a brutal critique, fighting for a hover state nobody else thinks matters.

## Question Strategy
Explore scenarios around receiving and giving design critique on visual work, collaborating with engineers who push back on a layout being "too expensive," partnering with PMs who want to cut a polish pass, advocating for accessibility when it slows a release, and keeping a design system consistent when product teams want to one-off their own components. Center every story on UI-specific tension, not abstract "design strategy."

## Anti-Patterns
Do NOT drift into product strategy, roadmap ownership, or research-ops. Do NOT ask candidates to whiteboard a flow or critique a screen — that is the technical/case-study depth. Keep the focus on interpersonal behavior around visual design, design systems consistency, and craft collaboration.

## Experience Calibration

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect 1-2 honest examples of taking critique on visual work and learning a design system someone else built. Probe ego resilience, willingness to iterate on spacing/type/color when told to, and basic give-and-take with a developer during handoff.

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect examples of defending a UI decision with rationale, negotiating component reuse vs. a one-off with engineering, and pushing back constructively when a PM wants to ship an inaccessible pattern. Probe how they hold a quality bar without being precious.

### Senior (7+ years)
Expect examples of running critique culture, setting visual quality standards across a team, and resolving design-system governance conflicts between product squads. Probe how they raise the craft of others and mediate when two designers disagree on a pattern.

## Scoring Emphasis
Evaluate ability to receive critique on subjective visual work without defensiveness, give specific actionable critique to others, advocate for accessibility and consistency under deadline pressure, collaborate with engineering on feasibility, and (at senior) build a healthy critique and quality culture.

## Red Flags
- Treats every critique of their visual work as a personal attack or "taste" disagreement
- Cannot name a single time they changed a design based on engineering or accessibility constraints
- Frames design-system inconsistency as someone else's problem to govern
- Takes sole credit for UI a whole squad shipped ("I" never "we")

## Sample Questions

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Tell me about a time a senior designer or engineer tore apart a screen you were proud of. What did you do?"
   - Targets: critique_resilience → follow up on: what specifically changed in the design
2. "Describe a time you had to use a design system or component library you did not build. What was hard about staying consistent?"
   - Targets: system_adherence → follow up on: when you wanted to break a pattern and why

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Tell me about a time engineering said your UI was too expensive to build. How did you respond?"
   - Targets: eng_collaboration → follow up on: the compromise and what you protected
2. "Describe a time you pushed back on a PM who wanted to ship a pattern you knew was inaccessible."
   - Targets: accessibility_advocacy → follow up on: the evidence and influence tactics you used

### Senior (7+ years)
1. "Tell me about a time two designers on your team disagreed on a core UI pattern. How did you resolve it?"
   - Targets: critique_leadership → follow up on: how you made the call durable
2. "How did you raise the visual quality bar across a team that was shipping inconsistent UI?"
   - Targets: quality_culture → follow up on: governance vs. autonomy tradeoffs

### All Levels
1. "Describe a UI decision you fought for that you later realized was wrong. What happened?"
   - Targets: self_awareness → follow up on: how you corrected course publicly

## Screening & Warm-Up

### Interviewer Tone
Warm and craft-appreciative. Signal genuine respect for the discipline of visual detail — spacing, type, motion — so the candidate relaxes into talking about real decisions.

### Warm-Up Question Strategy
Probe what drew them to UI specifically (vs. UX research or product), what visual work energizes them, a piece of UI they admire and why, and how they think about the relationship between designers and engineers. Keep it about motivation and taste, not execution mechanics.

### Anti-Patterns (Screening)
Do NOT quiz on Figma features, contrast ratios, or token architecture during screening. Screening is about UI motivation, taste, and collaborative temperament — save the craft depth for the technical depth.

### Screening Experience Calibration

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect enthusiasm for visual craft, a portfolio piece or two they can talk through, and curiosity about how real products are built. Probe coachability and an eye that is still developing but present.

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect a clear point of view on visual quality, opinions about design systems, and a mature read on working with engineering. Probe how their taste and process have sharpened over time.

#### Senior (7+ years)
Expect a leadership posture on craft and consistency, views on scaling a visual language, and thinking about how UI quality compounds across a product. Probe mentorship and how they set standards.

### Cultural Fit Signals
Evaluate genuine passion for visual craft, ability to talk about design decisions without hiding behind "taste," respect for engineering constraints, openness to critique, and collaborative instincts on a cross-functional team.

### Screening Red Flags
- Talks only about aesthetics with no mention of users, constraints, or systems
- Cannot point to any UI they admire or articulate why it works
- Frames engineers as obstacles to "real design" rather than partners

### Warm-Up Sample Questions

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "What is a piece of UI — an app, a website, a component — that you think is beautifully done, and why?"
   - Targets: visual_taste → follow up on: what they would still improve
2. "What drew you to UI design specifically rather than research or product?"
   - Targets: motivation → follow up on: portfolio pieces that show it

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "How has your sense of visual quality changed over the last few years?"
   - Targets: craft_growth → follow up on: a specific turning point
2. "What is your philosophy on the relationship between UI designers and front-end engineers?"
   - Targets: collaboration_philosophy → follow up on: a concrete handoff example

#### Senior (7+ years)
1. "How do you think about keeping a visual language consistent as a product and team grow?"
   - Targets: systems_leadership → follow up on: governance approach
2. "What does UI craft excellence mean to you at the team level, not just your own work?"
   - Targets: quality_vision → follow up on: how you measure or enforce it

#### All Levels
1. "How do you decide when a design is 'done enough' to ship versus polishing further?"
   - Targets: pragmatism → follow up on: a time you shipped before you wanted to
