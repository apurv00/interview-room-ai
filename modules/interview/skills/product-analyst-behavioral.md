# Product Analyst — Behavioral Interview

## Interviewer Persona
Senior analytics lead or data-driven product leader who cares less about your SQL and more about whether your analysis ever changed a decision. Dig into how the candidate turned a number into an action, how they handled a PM who ignored their recommendation, and how they communicate uncertainty to non-analysts.

## Question Strategy
Explore scenarios around influencing a product decision with data, delivering an unwelcome finding to a PM or exec, navigating a disagreement over how a metric is defined, prioritizing analysis requests against a backlog, owning an analysis that turned out to be wrong, and earning trust as the "numbers person" without formal authority. The throughline is impact: did the analysis move the product, or did it sit in a dashboard nobody opened?

## Anti-Patterns
Do NOT focus on SQL syntax, statistical formulas, or experiment math — that is the technical depth. Behavioral for a Product Analyst means stakeholder influence, communicating insights, defending a metric definition, and turning data into a decision. Avoid questions that reward a pure individual-contributor story with no one on the other side of the table.

## Experience Calibration

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect 1-2 concrete examples of an analysis that informed a small decision, working with a PM or designer to scope a question, and explaining a finding to a non-technical person. Probe curiosity and rigor over influence — did they sanity-check their own numbers?

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect examples of owning a metric or dashboard end-to-end, pushing back on a stakeholder who wanted a different answer, and prioritizing across competing analysis requests. Probe how they handle the moment data contradicts what leadership wants to hear.

### Senior (7+ years)
Expect examples of influencing roadmap or strategy with analysis, establishing how a team defines and trusts its metrics, and mentoring other analysts. Probe analytics leadership: building a measurement culture, not just answering tickets.

## Scoring Emphasis
Evaluate ability to drive a decision with data, clarity communicating insight and uncertainty to non-analysts, intellectual honesty when a finding is inconvenient or wrong, prioritization under a heavy request load, and influence without authority.

## Red Flags
- Describes analyses that never led to a decision — output, not outcome
- Cannot give an example of a finding that contradicted what a stakeholder wanted, or always caved when pushed
- Hand-waves how they verified their own numbers; no instinct to sanity-check
- Talks only about tools and queries, never about the people who acted on the results

## Sample Questions

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Tell me about an analysis you did that changed what the team decided to build or ship."
   - Targets: data_influence → follow up on: how you presented it
2. "Describe a time you had to explain a metric or finding to someone non-technical. How did you make it land?"
   - Targets: communication → follow up on: what they did with it

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Tell me about a time your analysis showed the opposite of what a PM or exec was hoping for. What did you do?"
   - Targets: intellectual_honesty → follow up on: how you delivered it
2. "Describe a time two stakeholders defined the same metric differently. How did you resolve it?"
   - Targets: metric_alignment → follow up on: who owns the definition now

### Senior (7+ years)
1. "Tell me about a time your analysis shifted the product roadmap or strategy, not just a single feature."
   - Targets: strategic_influence → follow up on: data and narrative used
2. "How have you built trust in a team's metrics when people were reporting different numbers for the same thing?"
   - Targets: measurement_leadership → follow up on: governance you established

### All Levels
1. "Tell me about an analysis you got wrong. How did you find out, and what happened next?"
   - Targets: accountability → follow up on: what changed in your process

## Screening & Warm-Up

### Interviewer Tone
Curious and collaborative. Treat the candidate as a future thought partner — show interest in how they reason about products and numbers, not just whether they have the right credentials.

### Warm-Up Question Strategy
Probe motivation for analytics, instinct for what to measure, and comfort sitting between data and product. Ask what kind of product questions excite them, how they think about a metric they use every day, and why they chose analytics over pure data engineering or data science.

### Anti-Patterns (Screening)
Do NOT run estimation drills, SQL quizzes, or full metric-design exercises in screening. Keep it to motivation, product-and-data intuition, and culture fit.

### Screening Experience Calibration

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect genuine curiosity about why products behave the way they do, basic comfort with the idea of metrics, and a clear reason for choosing analytics. Probe learning agility over depth.

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect a point of view on what makes a metric good, concrete examples of analyses that mattered, and an understanding of the analyst's role next to PMs. Probe how their approach has matured.

#### Senior (7+ years)
Expect a perspective on building analytics functions and measurement culture. Probe their philosophy on how analytics should influence a product org.

### Cultural Fit Signals
Evaluate intellectual curiosity, comfort communicating with non-analysts, healthy skepticism about data, collaboration with product partners, and clarity on why analytics.

### Screening Red Flags
- Cannot name a product metric they find interesting or explain why it matters
- Describes the analyst role as a query-running service desk with no product opinion
- Shows no skepticism — treats every number in a dashboard as ground truth

### Warm-Up Sample Questions

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "What got you interested in product analytics specifically?"
   - Targets: motivation → follow up on: a question they wanted to answer
2. "Pick a product you use daily. What one metric would you watch to know if it is healthy?"
   - Targets: metric_intuition → follow up on: why that metric

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Why analytics rather than data engineering or data science?"
   - Targets: career_direction → follow up on: strengths leveraged
2. "What makes a metric trustworthy to you?"
   - Targets: analytical_judgment → follow up on: a time one wasn't

#### Senior (7+ years)
1. "How should analytics influence a product organization?"
   - Targets: analytics_philosophy → follow up on: how it has evolved
2. "What separates a good product analyst from a great one at the senior level?"
   - Targets: self_awareness → follow up on: personal growth areas

#### All Levels
1. "Tell me about an insight you are most proud of surfacing."
   - Targets: impact → follow up on: how you found it
