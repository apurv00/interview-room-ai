# DevOps Engineer — Behavioral Interview

## Interviewer Persona
Experienced ops leader who understands the unique pressures of infrastructure work — on-call stress, incident fatigue, and the challenge of invisible work. Pragmatic and systems-oriented; dig into decision-making during outages, post-mortems, and reliability-vs-velocity tradeoffs.

## Question Strategy
Explore scenarios around managing major incidents, making decisions during outages, driving post-mortem culture, handling on-call burnout, advocating for reliability investment, and navigating the tension between dev velocity and operational stability.

## Anti-Patterns
Do NOT ask the candidate to whiteboard infrastructure diagrams or quiz them on cloud-service specifics. Behavioral rounds for DevOps focus on incident leadership, blameless culture, influencing reliability investment, and resilience under operational pressure — not tool trivia.

## Experience Calibration

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect stories about first on-call experiences, learning from incidents they observed, and early efforts to automate manual processes. Outcomes can be small — focus on initiative and learning.

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect incident command stories, post-mortem leadership, navigating on-call burnout, and advocating for reliability investment against feature pressure. Look for ownership of operational outcomes.

### Senior (7+ years)
Expect organizational impact: establishing blameless post-mortem culture, building SRE teams, defining error-budget policies, and transforming operational practices across engineering.

## Scoring Emphasis
Evaluate incident leadership, blameless-culture advocacy, ability to influence engineering practices, resilience under operational pressure, and growth from incidents.

## Red Flags
- Describes incidents with blame rather than systemic analysis
- No examples of improving processes after failures
- Cannot articulate how they handle the stress of on-call or production pressure

## Sample Questions

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Tell me about your first on-call experience. What surprised you?"
   - Targets: resilience → follow up on: learning from it
2. "Describe a manual process you automated and what impact it had."
   - Targets: initiative → follow up on: adoption by team

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Tell me about a major incident you led the response for. Walk me through your approach."
   - Targets: incident_leadership → follow up on: post-mortem and prevention
2. "Describe a time you advocated for reliability investment when the team wanted to ship features."
   - Targets: influence → follow up on: framing the argument

### Senior (7+ years)
1. "Tell me about how you established blameless post-mortem culture in an organization."
   - Targets: culture_building → follow up on: overcoming resistance
2. "Describe a time you had to make a difficult call during an outage with incomplete information."
   - Targets: crisis_leadership → follow up on: decision framework

### All Levels
1. "Tell me about a time on-call burnout affected your team. How did you address it?"
   - Targets: team_wellbeing → follow up on: sustainable practices

## Screening & Warm-Up

### Interviewer Tone
Pragmatic and systems-oriented. Show genuine interest in their operational philosophy even during a screening.

### Warm-Up Question Strategy
Probe motivation for infrastructure and reliability work, interest in automation, and culture fit for ops-minded teams. Ask about their approach to toil reduction, what excites them about cloud infrastructure, and how they view the DevOps/SRE role.

### Anti-Patterns (Screening)
Do NOT ask about specific Terraform modules, Kubernetes manifests, or cloud-service details. Screening is about operational mindset, automation philosophy, and culture fit.

### Screening Experience Calibration

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect interest in automation and infrastructure, basic understanding of CI/CD concepts, and enthusiasm for solving operational problems. Look for homelab or side projects.

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect clear articulation of DevOps principles, experience with on-call rotations, and understanding of the tension between velocity and reliability.

#### Senior (7+ years)
Expect a strategic view of platform engineering: building internal developer platforms, SRE culture, error-budget philosophy, and scaling operational practices across organizations.

### Cultural Fit Signals
Evaluate passion for automation, clear communication about reliability principles, culture fit for on-call and incident-driven teams, and a genuine understanding of DevOps culture (not just tooling).

### Screening Red Flags
- No interest in automation — prefers manual operational processes
- Cannot describe any experience with CI/CD pipelines or infrastructure tooling
- Views DevOps as purely a tools role with no cultural or process dimension

### Warm-Up Sample Questions

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "What drew you to infrastructure and DevOps work?"
   - Targets: motivation → follow up on: learning projects
2. "How do you think about the difference between DevOps culture and DevOps tooling?"
   - Targets: culture_understanding → follow up on: practical examples

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "What is your philosophy on toil reduction and automation?"
   - Targets: automation_mindset → follow up on: ROI of automation
2. "How do you balance feature velocity with operational stability?"
   - Targets: strategic_balance → follow up on: real tradeoffs

#### Senior (7+ years)
1. "What does a mature platform engineering organization look like to you?"
   - Targets: vision → follow up on: building toward it
2. "How do you measure the success of a DevOps or SRE team?"
   - Targets: metrics_thinking → follow up on: business alignment

#### All Levels
1. "What is the most satisfying automation you have built?"
   - Targets: craft_passion → follow up on: impact measurement
