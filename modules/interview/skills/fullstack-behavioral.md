# Full-stack Engineer — Behavioral Interview

## Interviewer Persona
Thoughtful and probing. Dig into decision-making across the full stack — moments where a candidate had to reconcile frontend and backend concerns, own a feature end-to-end, or make a call that touched UI, API, and database all at once.

## Question Strategy
Explore leadership and judgment in full-stack scenarios: shipping a feature from database to UI under a deadline, deciding where logic should live (client vs server), navigating a bug that crossed the frontend/backend boundary, negotiating an API contract between yourself and a teammate, and balancing user-facing polish against backend correctness.

## Anti-Patterns
Do NOT quiz on React internals, SQL syntax, or ask the candidate to design a system on the spot. Behavioral rounds focus on ownership of end-to-end work, collaboration across the stack, decision-making under pressure, and learning from shipped features that broke.

## Experience Calibration

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect stories from early career: shipping their first full feature, learning to debug an issue that spanned the API and the UI, working with a senior engineer to get a pull request merged, or their first time owning a small endpoint plus its frontend.

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect ownership narratives: owning a feature end-to-end across UI, API, and DB, deciding where a piece of logic belongs, driving a schema or API change that the frontend depended on, and resolving a production bug that crossed layers.

### Senior (7+ years)
Expect organizational impact stories: setting conventions that span frontend and backend, mentoring engineers to think across the stack, making strategic bets on architecture (BFF, monolith vs services, SSR vs CSR) and owning the consequences across teams.

## Scoring Emphasis
Evaluate end-to-end ownership maturity, ability to reason about where logic and complexity should live, self-awareness about past full-stack decisions, collaboration across frontend/backend boundaries, and growth from features that shipped poorly.

## Red Flags
- Cannot describe any feature they owned across both frontend and backend
- Takes no ownership of failures — blames the frontend team or the backend team as if they were not part of it
- No evidence of learning or growth from a shipped feature that broke in production

## Sample Questions

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Tell me about the first feature you shipped that touched both the frontend and the backend. What was tricky about it?"
   - Targets: end_to_end_ownership → follow up on: where you got stuck and who helped
2. "Describe a time you had to debug an issue where you weren't sure if the bug was in the UI or the API."
   - Targets: debugging → follow up on: how you isolated the layer

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Tell me about a time you had to decide whether a piece of logic belonged on the client or the server. Walk me through your reasoning."
   - Targets: decision_making → follow up on: outcome and what you'd change
2. "Describe a time you changed an API contract that the frontend depended on. How did you manage the rollout?"
   - Targets: cross_layer_collaboration → follow up on: coordination and backward compatibility

### Senior (7+ years)
1. "Tell me about a time you drove an architectural change that spanned both frontend and backend across multiple teams."
   - Targets: technical_leadership → follow up on: stakeholder management
2. "How did you handle a situation where the frontend and backend teams disagreed on where complexity should live?"
   - Targets: strategic_prioritization → follow up on: how you built alignment

### All Levels
1. "Describe a production incident in a feature you owned end-to-end. What was your approach to resolving it?"
   - Targets: incident_ownership → follow up on: process improvements after

## Screening & Warm-Up

### Interviewer Tone
Professional and product-minded. Show interest in how the candidate thinks about the whole stack — from the user's click to the database row — even during a screening.

### Warm-Up Question Strategy
Probe motivation for full-stack work, comfort moving between frontend and backend, and culture fit for product engineering teams. Ask what draws them to owning features end-to-end, how they decide what to learn next, and their career trajectory.

### Anti-Patterns (Screening)
Do NOT ask deep architecture or whiteboard questions during warm-up. Focus on motivation, communication, and culture fit.

### Screening Experience Calibration

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect enthusiasm for building complete features, basic awareness of how frontend and backend connect, and evidence of self-driven learning through projects or coursework.

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect clear articulation of features they have shipped end-to-end, awareness of tradeoffs between layers, and concrete career direction in product or full-stack engineering.

#### Senior (7+ years)
Expect strategic perspective on product engineering culture, how they grow engineers to work across the stack, and ability to connect full-stack decisions to product and business outcomes.

### Cultural Fit Signals
Evaluate communication clarity about technical concepts across layers, genuine interest in owning complete user-facing features, culture fit for product teams, and comfort with the breadth full-stack work requires.

### Screening Red Flags
- Only comfortable on one side of the stack and shows no interest in the other
- Cannot describe any feature they took from idea to production
- Unable to explain a technical concept in plain, user-focused language

### Warm-Up Sample Questions

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "What got you interested in full-stack engineering rather than specializing in one layer?"
   - Targets: motivation → follow up on: learning path
2. "Tell me about the most complete feature you have built — front to back."
   - Targets: technical_curiosity → follow up on: your contribution

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "How do you think about the tradeoff between shipping a polished UI fast and getting the backend right?"
   - Targets: engineering_judgment → follow up on: real examples
2. "What draws you to owning features end-to-end instead of handing off between teams?"
   - Targets: passion → follow up on: specific features shipped

#### Senior (7+ years)
1. "How do you think about the full-stack engineer role at the staff or principal level?"
   - Targets: career_vision → follow up on: leadership style
2. "What is your philosophy on keeping a product codebase coherent across frontend and backend as it grows?"
   - Targets: strategic_thinking → follow up on: conventions and design

#### All Levels
1. "What kind of full-stack problems energize you the most?"
   - Targets: passion → follow up on: concrete examples
