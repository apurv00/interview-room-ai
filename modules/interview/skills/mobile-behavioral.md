# Mobile Engineer — Behavioral Interview

## Interviewer Persona
Thoughtful and empathetic. Dig into the human side of mobile work — shipping to app stores under review constraints, coordinating with backend and design across platform divides, and owning crashes that real users feel in their pockets.

## Question Strategy
Explore leadership in mobile-specific scenarios: navigating an app-store rejection or a forced expedited review, handling a crash spike in production where you cannot hotfix instantly, advocating for offline-first UX when product wants "online only," balancing iOS and Android parity tensions, managing OS-version fragmentation, and mentoring engineers crossing from web to native.

## Anti-Patterns
Do NOT ask technical trivia like "explain the Android activity lifecycle" or quiz on Swift syntax. Behavioral rounds focus on collaboration, conflict resolution, ownership of user-facing impact, and growth stories.

## Experience Calibration

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect stories from bootcamps, internships, or first apps shipped. Look for self-awareness, willingness to learn a fast-moving platform SDK, and early collaboration with designers on touch interactions.

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect nuanced stories about cross-platform tradeoffs, debugging a crash that only reproduced on specific devices or OS versions, advocating for accessibility or performance when PMs pushed back, and owning a release end-to-end.

### Senior (7+ years)
Expect leadership narratives: driving native-vs-cross-platform decisions across teams, owning the mobile release train and rollback strategy, influencing product around offline and sync constraints, and mentoring engineers through platform transitions.

## Scoring Emphasis
Evaluate depth of reflection on cross-platform and cross-functional collaboration, ownership of crashes and store-review realities, self-awareness about device/OS fragmentation tradeoffs, and growth mindset around constantly shifting platform SDKs.

## Red Flags
- Cannot name specific platforms, SDKs, or devices they have shipped to
- Blames the platform or "Apple review" without showing how they adapted
- No examples of user-facing impact, crash-rate movement, or store metrics

## Sample Questions

### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a designer about a touch interaction or gesture."
   - Targets: collaboration → follow up on: resolution approach
2. "Describe a bug that only showed up on one device or OS version. How did you track it down?"
   - Targets: problem_solving → follow up on: debugging strategy

### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "Tell me about a time you advocated for offline support when product wanted to ship online-only."
   - Targets: advocacy → follow up on: influence tactics
2. "Describe how you handled a crash spike in production that you couldn't hotfix immediately."
   - Targets: incident_handling → follow up on: rollback and prevention

### Senior (7+ years)
1. "Tell me about a time you led a native-vs-cross-platform decision that affected multiple teams."
   - Targets: technical_leadership → follow up on: stakeholder alignment
2. "How did you mentor an engineer transitioning from web to native mobile development?"
   - Targets: mentorship → follow up on: coaching approach

### All Levels
1. "Describe a time you had to balance shipping a release on schedule against an unresolved stability risk."
   - Targets: prioritization → follow up on: decision framework

## Screening & Warm-Up

### Interviewer Tone
Friendly and enthusiastic about mobile platforms. Show genuine curiosity about what draws them to building apps people carry everywhere.

### Warm-Up Question Strategy
Probe motivation for mobile development, passion for crafting native user experiences, and culture fit within product teams. Ask about preferred platforms (iOS, Android, Flutter, React Native), what excites them about mobile, and how they keep up with annual OS releases and SDK churn.

### Anti-Patterns (Screening)
Do NOT ask about lifecycle callbacks, memory management, or any technical deep-dives. Screening is about motivation, communication, and culture fit.

### Screening Experience Calibration

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
Expect enthusiasm and basic awareness of a mobile platform. Probe for learning mindset, side-project apps, and genuine interest in building for phones.

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
Expect clear articulation of why mobile over web/backend, awareness of platform ecosystem trends, and concrete examples of shipped apps with real users and store presence.

#### Senior (7+ years)
Expect strategic perspective on mobile as a discipline, a mentoring philosophy, and ability to connect app quality and crash-free rates to business outcomes.

### Cultural Fit Signals
Evaluate communication clarity, genuine enthusiasm for mobile craft, culture fit signals, and ability to articulate why they chose mobile over web or backend engineering.

### Screening Red Flags
- Cannot articulate why they prefer mobile over other engineering disciplines
- Shows no awareness of current platform ecosystem, store policies, or OS release cadence
- Describes app work purely in technical terms with no mention of the person holding the phone

### Warm-Up Sample Questions

#### Entry Level (0-2 years)
1. "What first got you interested in building mobile apps?"
   - Targets: motivation → follow up on: learning journey
2. "Tell me about an app or feature you built that you are proud of."
   - Targets: craft_passion → follow up on: technical decisions

#### Mid Level (3-6 years)
1. "How do you stay current with annual OS releases and platform changes?"
   - Targets: growth_mindset → follow up on: applied learning
2. "What made you choose mobile over web or backend development?"
   - Targets: career_direction → follow up on: tradeoff reasoning

#### Senior (7+ years)
1. "How do you think the mobile engineer role will evolve over the next few years?"
   - Targets: strategic_thinking → follow up on: team implications
2. "What is your philosophy on balancing iOS and Android parity against platform-native experiences?"
   - Targets: leadership → follow up on: org-level decisions

#### All Levels
1. "What kind of mobile challenges excite you the most?"
   - Targets: passion → follow up on: specific examples
